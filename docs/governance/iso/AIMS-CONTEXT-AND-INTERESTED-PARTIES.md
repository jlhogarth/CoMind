# CoMind AIMS Context and Interested Parties Register

Date: 2026-10-10
Status: Draft management-system context. Requires periodic management review as products, markets and stakeholders change.

## Organizational context

CoMind is building governed persistent cognition and a Virtual Employee Foundry in which AI-enabled components may retain memory, coordinate work, use tools, make recommendations, and eventually execute bounded actions under explicit authority. This creates opportunities for continuity, productivity and institutional memory, but also creates material risks around autonomy, provenance, privacy, security, availability, cost, supplier dependence, human oversight and false claims of system state.

The AIMS must therefore integrate with software engineering, database/security controls, project management, provider governance, FinOps, continuity/recovery, observability and human decision authority rather than operate as a detached compliance layer.

## Internal context factors

Material internal factors include:

- early-stage architecture with significant capability still in isolated/repository state rather than live production;
- parallel development streams for Foundry autonomy, durable continuity and runtime performance;
- reliance on persistent memory/provenance and future agentic execution;
- explicit C0-C4 authority and T0-T3 engineering governance concepts;
- requirement that delegated work not disappear silently;
- limited organizational size, which may make strict role separation difficult and require compensating independent review;
- rapid technical change and frequent architecture evolution;
- need to control paid provider usage and runtime overhead;
- current live database security posture that is not yet adequate for production Virtual Employee autonomy;
- strong requirement for evidence-backed claims and no-placeholder development.

## External context factors

Material external factors include:

- fast-changing foundation-model/provider capabilities, pricing, terms and safety behavior;
- evolving AI standards, security guidance and regulatory expectations;
- cloud/database/source-control/connector supplier concentration;
- adversarial techniques targeting prompt, goal, memory, RAG, tool and agent authority boundaries;
- certification/assurance expectations from future enterprise customers;
- privacy/security expectations for persistent personal or organizational memory;
- availability and continuity of external APIs and SaaS dependencies;
- interoperability evolution such as MCP/A2A and associated supply-chain/authorization risks.

## Requirements-register rule

Interested-party expectations become mandatory AIMS requirements only when they are applicable through law, regulation, contract, approved policy, management decision, standard/certification scope, or accepted product requirement. ISO may index and flag candidate requirements but may not invent legal obligations.

## Review triggers

Review this context and interested-party register when:

- a new product/use case enters scope;
- CoMind enters a new market/geography or regulated domain;
- production autonomy materially expands;
- a significant supplier/model/data-flow change occurs;
- a material incident or audit finding changes risk assumptions;
- certification scope is proposed or changed;
- applicable standards/regulation materially change;
- organizational ownership or governance structure changes.

## Current explicit gaps

- target certification legal entity wording not yet approved;
- target markets/geographies and complete legal obligations not yet fixed;
- enterprise/customer contractual requirements are not yet represented as a controlled set;
- privacy/legal retention requirements are not yet translated into a final evidence-retention schedule;
- named AIMS management/risk/control/audit owners are not yet formally designated.

These are management-system inputs to resolve, not reasons to fabricate placeholder requirements.
