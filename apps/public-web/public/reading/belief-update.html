How a belief should change

A remembered claim should respond to evidence, preserve its history, and remain open to correction.

Public behavioral specification · Based on CoMind GPC Belief Confidence Update Rule and Contradiction Resolution Protocol, version 1.0, 18 February 2026 · Design requirements, not a claim that every mechanism is deployed.

From statement to evidence

Suppose a project record says a service will launch in June. Later, a signed decision moves it to September. A useful system needs to know which statement was first, which is current, who made the change, and what evidence supports it. Retaining both statements without a resolution can produce a confident but false answer.

The public specification describes a belief statement with a domain, confidence, status, and provenance. New evidence has a source, time, direction, and assessed reliability. Material changes should leave an audit trail. Confidence should respond to evidence while avoiding abrupt swings caused by wording alone.

A contradiction is a case to resolve

Two statements can conflict because one is outdated, because they apply in different contexts, or because the evidence remains incomplete. Possible resolutions include narrowing a statement's scope, marking it obsolete, merging or splitting claims, adjusting confidence, or deferring a decision. A high impact conflict calls for human review.

That sequence protects a distinction: evidence can recommend a change, while an authorized decision determines whether and how a consequential belief is promoted.

What this publication discloses

This public edition explains observable behavior and decision boundaries. It does not publish internal mathematics, coefficients, thresholds, prompts, schema, or enforcement mechanics. Those details are outside the approved public specification.
