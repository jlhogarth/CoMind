A research agenda for human and AI continuity

What would have to be true for a persistent human and AI relationship to improve work over time?

Research agenda · Source: HAI-006, version 0.4, 30 October 2026 · Hypotheses are provisional; no human efficacy result is claimed.

The question

CoMind studies seven connected domains: interaction, continuity, understanding, adaptation, collaboration, integration, and co-development. The names organize questions. They do not establish seven scientifically validated stages or a universal order.

One proposed benefit of continuity is less time spent reconstructing goals and decisions. The competing risk is equally important: a system can preserve an outdated assumption and repeat it with more confidence. Our research asks whether governed continuity improves resumption quality while reducing those errors.

What we would measure

A longitudinal task can be paused after a decision, a correction, and an unresolved commitment. On resumption, an evaluator can ask whether the system recalls the current decision, rejects the superseded one, preserves the open commitment, and identifies the source for each. Accuracy and effort matter together.

  • Task outcome: accuracy, completion, time, rework, and cost.
  • Continuity: resume accuracy, correction persistence, stale assumptions, and provenance coverage.
  • Human agency: control over retention, correction, deletion, and consequential action.
  • Relationship: calibrated trust and evidence that both human practice and AI support improve, rather than mere familiarity.

How the idea could fail

If continuity adds overhead without improving longitudinal outcomes, its central place in the model should shrink. If people collaborate well without retained context, the proposed ordering should change. If co-development cannot be distinguished from practice effects, ordinary personalization, or a model upgrade, it should not be treated as a separate established construct.

This is why a research agenda begins with possible disconfirmation. A persuasive label is not a measurement instrument, and a synthetic test is not evidence of benefit to people.
