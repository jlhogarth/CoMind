What research supports a human and AI relationship?

A map of established ideas, CoMind extensions, and claims that still need evidence.

Literature reconciliation · Source: HAI-007, version 1.1, 30 October 2026 · This is a research synthesis, not empirical validation of CoMind.

A map with uneven evidence

Interaction and collaboration have substantial research traditions. Shared mental models and adaptive automation offer useful ways to examine understanding and adaptation. Longitudinal continuity and reciprocal co-development are less settled, particularly across real organizations and longer time spans.

CoMind keeps seven domains as a readable vocabulary, but interprets them as a partially ordered, multidimensional profile. A person and system can improve in one domain while remaining weak in another. A single maturity score would hide that variation.

Where CoMind extends the question

Remembering past exchanges is only one part of continuity. CoMind asks whether a retained statement carries provenance, whether a correction replaces an old assumption, whether its use is authorized, and whether a person can reverse an adaptation. These are design requirements and research questions. The literature does not establish that CoMind has already delivered their promised human benefit.

Co-development is a candidate construct: improvement on both sides of the working relationship over time. A study must separate it from a model upgrade, growing user familiarity, and more frequent use before claiming a distinct effect.

A useful caution

Putting a person and an AI in the same workflow does not guarantee a better result. The research question is when their complementary strengths, coordination, verification, and responsibility boundaries outperform the strongest participant working alone. Evidence about a bounded task should not be stretched into a universal claim about a relationship.

Next questions

What would count as a correction that remains effective months later? How much context is useful before privacy and stale assumptions outweigh the gain? The research agenda frames these as falsifiable questions; the pilot protocol specifies the first controlled comparison.
