# CoMind Artificial Intelligence Management Policy Draft

Date: 2026-10-10
Status: Draft management policy. Requires formal management approval before being represented as controlled organizational policy.

## Policy statement

CoMind will design, develop, deploy and operate AI-enabled systems through governed, evidence-based processes that preserve human accountability, bounded authority, security, privacy, reliability, recoverability, transparency of system state, and continual improvement.

CoMind's governing epistemic rule is:

> Never substitute plausibility, confidence, verbosity, or user agreement for epistemic justification. No Hallucinations. No Lies.

This rule does not claim that AI errors can be eliminated. It requires CoMind to distinguish observation, evidence, inference, uncertainty, proposal, implementation, deployment and verified operation rather than presenting one as another.

## Commitments

CoMind commits to:

1. maintain an AIMS scope, objectives, roles, controlled policies and evidence appropriate to organizational context and AI risk;
2. identify and assess AI-related risks and impacts before consequential capability expansion and when material change triggers occur;
3. apply least privilege, explicit authority, bounded delegation and human approval proportionate to consequence;
4. maintain traceable identity, provenance, authorization, cost, execution and recovery evidence for consequential Virtual Employee actions;
5. secure sensitive data, credentials, memory, retrieval systems, tools, connectors and supplier interfaces;
6. define intended and prohibited uses for material AI systems and reassess when use materially changes;
7. manage external models, platforms and suppliers as dependencies with risk, contractual, continuity and change considerations;
8. test relevant failure, misuse, prompt-injection, privilege, recovery and inter-agent scenarios before expanding autonomy;
9. preserve the ability to pause, revoke, contain, recover and investigate governed AI operations;
10. maintain human accountability for risk acceptance, material exceptions, certification claims and organizational management decisions;
11. prohibit self-certification and self-approval by Virtual Employees;
12. measure reliability, cost, security and governance overhead, including impact on interactive performance;
13. retain sufficient evidence to audit material decisions while minimizing unnecessary sensitive content;
14. investigate incidents/nonconformities and verify corrective-action effectiveness;
15. maintain competence, awareness, internal audit, management review and continual improvement appropriate to the AIMS;
16. comply with applicable legal, regulatory, contractual and standards obligations once identified for the actual product, market, geography and use case.

## Authority and human oversight

AI systems and Virtual Employees receive only explicitly granted authority. Capability availability does not itself establish permission to use it. Missing or unverifiable authority must fail closed for consequential action.

No agent or group of agents may create authority by consensus, override a human approval boundary, accept organizational risk outside delegated authority, alter its own privileges, or suppress evidence required for oversight.

## Evidence and claims

Control claims must identify evidence state. Repository existence, design intent, historical testing and live operation are distinct states. Unsupported claims of certification, compliance, deployment, testing or successful completion are prohibited.

Failures, dissent, uncertainty and negative evidence are retained when material to the control or decision. Evidence must not be selectively curated to manufacture a favorable governance outcome.

## Security, privacy and safety

CoMind applies defense in depth to AI-specific and traditional software risks. Material threats include prompt/goal hijacking, tool misuse, privilege abuse, supply-chain compromise, unexpected code execution, sensitive-data disclosure, poisoned knowledge/memory, unbounded resource use, cross-context leakage and unsafe autonomous behavior.

Data collection and retention should be minimized to what is necessary for product function, evidence, safety, legal and operational needs. Secrets and credentials must not be embedded in governance records.

## Suppliers and models

External providers are governed through selection, configuration, data-flow analysis, access control, monitoring, supplier assessment, change management and contingency planning. CoMind will not represent provider internals as controlled by CoMind when they are not.

Material model/provider/version changes require reassessment proportionate to impact.

## Performance and proportionality

Governance must be strong enough to control risk without silently making the system unusable. Runtime controls on critical paths should be deterministic and bounded where feasible. Heavy evidence analysis and audit preparation should be asynchronous unless a pre-execution decision is required.

## Incident and corrective action

Material AI incidents, unauthorized actions, evidence-integrity failures, security/privacy breaches, repeated control failures and unsupported compliance/certification representations trigger investigation and corrective action proportionate to impact. Recovery must not erase evidence needed for investigation.

## Continual improvement

AIMS objectives, risk treatment, evidence quality, controls, supplier posture, performance and incidents are reviewed on defined cadences and after material events. Standards changes are evaluated before adoption rather than silently altering acceptance criteria.

## Certification claims

CoMind may implement ISO/IEC 42001 practices without being certified. Any claim of ISO/IEC 42001 certification or equivalent conformity requires appropriate evidence and management authorization, including independent conformity assessment where certification is claimed.

## Approval

Formal approval must record the accountable management approver, approval date, effective date, review date/version and superseded policy version. Until that approval exists, this file remains a draft.
