Research direction / Evidence

Governed reasoning

An answer should make its basis visible enough for a person to judge it. Confidence, fluency, and a citation are not substitutes for evidence that actually supports the claim.

Public research overview · An exploration of questions and design principles, not a published paper or product claim.

Four distinct steps

  1. Observation: what a source or test directly showed.
  2. Inference: what may follow from those observations.
  3. Decision: the choice a person or authorized process makes.
  4. Verification: what later evidence shows about the result.

Keeping these steps separate helps expose gaps before they become confident statements.

A small example

A deployment tool reports success. That is an observation about the upload. It does not yet prove that visitors can load the page. A live HTTP request and a browser readback provide separate verification. If an early request fails, the record should preserve that failure and the subsequent outcome.

Where human authority belongs

Evidence can inform a choice, but it does not grant permission to publish private information or change a production system. The authorization boundary should be explicit, especially when a decision affects people, money, security, or public claims.

Questions for further research

  • Can a reader trace each important assertion to its actual source?
  • What would change the conclusion?
  • Who is authorized to decide, and who independently checks the outcome?
