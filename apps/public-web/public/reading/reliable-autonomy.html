Research direction / Execution

Reliable autonomy

Autonomous work should be bounded, observable, recoverable, and accountable. A task is complete when its outcome can be checked, not when an agent says it is done.

Public research overview · An exploration of questions and design principles, not a published paper or product claim.

Start with a bounded assignment

A clear task names its objective, allowed resources, approval limits, and stopping condition. Those limits let the system make routine progress while preserving a person’s control over consequential actions.

Make work legible while it runs

Useful records distinguish queued, running, blocked, failed, and completed states. They identify the current owner and the last verified event. A heartbeat can show that a process is alive, but it cannot prove that the intended work advanced.

Treat failure as part of the design

Retries need bounds. A stalled task needs a recovery path and an escalation point. Evidence should show whether a retry created a duplicate effect, whether the work resumed, and what remains unresolved.

Completion test

What changed, what test read it back, and where is the durable record? If those questions have no answer, the outcome remains unverified.

Questions for further research

  • How can a process prove progress without confusing activity with completion?
  • What should happen after a partial success?
  • How can independent checks detect a false success report?
