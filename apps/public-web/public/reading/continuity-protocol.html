How to test governed continuity

A controlled comparison of starting over, retaining history, and retaining corrected, sourced context.

Pilot protocol · Source: HAI-009, version 1.0, 30 October 2026 · Synthetic instrumentation preflight; no real participants or measured human benefit.

Three conditions

  1. Fresh session: the system receives a standard resumption prompt but no retained longitudinal state.
  2. Memory only: earlier context is available without guaranteed currentness, correction, provenance, or unresolved-work controls.
  3. Governed continuity: retained context is checked for currentness and correction, linked to its source, and preserves unresolved commitments.

The comparison is provider neutral. It isolates the quality of continuity from the quantity of remembered text.

What the test asks

Each fictional task history contains known ground truth: a decision, a changed fact, a correction, and unfinished work. The evaluator asks whether a resumed system can retrieve the right state without reviving the superseded one. Measures include resume accuracy, correction persistence, stale-assumption persistence, unresolved-commitment recovery, provenance, and context reconstruction effort.

Reference states are versioned and checked against a content digest. A test that silently changes its answer key after observing output would not be a meaningful comparison.

What passing would and would not mean

Early fixtures exercise the scorer and instrumentation with deliberately chosen cases. They show whether the measurement machinery behaves as specified. They do not show that an actual user received better support or that a real system outperformed a comparator. A later human study would require its own participant safeguards, consent, analysis plan, and review.

The current public claim is therefore narrow: CoMind has specified a testable protocol. Its hypotheses remain hypotheses.
