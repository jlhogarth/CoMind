A map of the ideas

Three questions connect CoMind’s research: what remains useful across time, what makes a conclusion accountable, and what proves that an action happened?

Public research overview · An exploration of questions and design principles, not a published paper or product claim.

1. Continuity makes learning cumulative

Imagine returning to a complex project after a month. A useful assistant can recover the objective, the decisions, and the reasons behind them. It also needs to recognize what may have changed. Memory becomes valuable when it preserves context with its source and date, and when stale assumptions can be challenged.

Explore cognitive continuity →

2. Reasoning makes conclusions inspectable

Remembered context can be mistaken. A responsible system should distinguish what was observed, what was inferred, and what remains uncertain. A person should be able to trace a recommendation to evidence and retain authority over consequential choices.

Explore governed reasoning →

3. Execution makes promises testable

A convincing plan does not establish that a task ran. Reliable autonomous work needs a bounded assignment, visible state, an outcome, and a way to recover or escalate failures. The evidence should let someone independently answer what happened.

Explore reliable autonomy →

The connecting loop

Continuity supplies relevant context. Governed reasoning checks whether that context supports a decision. Reliable execution acts within the approved boundary and returns evidence. That evidence becomes new context, ready to be checked again. Each link can fail, so none should be treated as automatic proof of the next.

A question to carry forward

When an AI says it remembers, knows, or completed something, what record would let you verify that claim?
